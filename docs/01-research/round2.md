# Research Round 2 — Value Chain, Users, Japan, Fishpond (2026-11-05)

Method: deep-research workflow covering 5 angles and 22 sources. 25 claims verified with 3 adversarial votes each: 23 confirmed, 2 rejected. After merging duplicates they make 10 findings.
**Coverage is uneven.** Angle 5 (PH agritech landscape and who pays) produced no verified claims, so it needs Round 3.

## A. Copra value chain
1. **Visual moisture grading costs farmers money.** 82% of 400 copra sales (2005, 10 regions incl. Region V) were graded for moisture by eye. Copra graded by eye sold for ₱17.21/kg; copra checked with a meter sold for ₱18.53/kg, about 7.7% more. 85.5% of sales took a 14–22% moisture deduction. *(medium)*
   - Rivera & Lantican, CORD 2010 — https://journal.coconutcommunity.org/index.php/journalicc/article/download/133/120
2. **Farmers are price takers, and pooling volume helps.** 89% said the buyer sets the price. Negotiated prices were higher (₱17.97 vs ₱17.37). The authors estimate a 10 t seller gets ₱0.50–0.80/kg more and recommend stronger farmer organizations. *(medium, same source)*
3. **CFIDP co-op grants (RA 11524).** CDA–PCA CocoFarmCoop gives up to ₱250k per purpose to coconut farmer co-ops, plus registration subsidies and help enrolling in the National Coconut Farmers Registry. The program is active in CDA Region V. *(high)*
   - https://cda.gov.ph/region-4b/cda-pca-partnership-implementation-on-mimaropa-region-coconut-farmers-organization-and-development-through-cooperatives-cocofarmcoop
   - https://cda.gov.ph/region-5/cda-continuously-capacitates-coconut-farmers-cooperatives-and-organizations
4. ⚠️ **Refuted in Round 3 (1-2): do not rely on the accounting/audit part.** Market matching survived. Original claim: **CFIDP's planned co-op support includes a computerized accounting system, computers, digital market matching, and subsidies for financial statements and audits.** These are planned items from the MIMAROPA rollout, not confirmed in Bicol. This is the strongest verified lead on who might pay. *(medium)*

**Rejected, do not use:** "94% sell to traders while millers pay more" (1-2) and "buyer credit lowers price" (0-3).

## B. Connectivity and users (Bicol)
5. **Ligao City, Albay, 2026, n=90 farmers:** 34% had reliable internet and 80% had unstable connections. Barriers: hard to understand online information (82%), lack of technical skill (81%), trust (76%), data cost (74%), outdated phones (74%), too little local-language content (72%). The author recommends low-bandwidth tools, SMS, and content in English, Filipino and Bicol, including audio. *(medium; not Masbate)*
   - https://luxveritatis.ust-legazpi.edu.ph/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/REVISED.-CABREDO.pdf
6. **PIDS DP 2023-29:** advisory apps are widespread but little used. Other digital tools are still prototypes. The digital divide can be bridged through *community organizing* and technology rental. *(medium)*
   - https://ideas.repec.org/p/phd/dpaper/dp_2023-29.html

## C. Japan lessons
7. **JA model:** a multipurpose co-op that bundles joint marketing, input supply, credit, insurance and farm guidance. It does two-way matching: sales proposals to buyers based on what members produce, and production proposals to members based on what buyers want. *(medium; JA Zenchu 2012 deck)*
   - https://www.fao.org/fileadmin/user_upload/fao_ilo/pdf/Presentations/_Final__Presentation_from_JAZenchu_Japan.pdf
8. **JA bookkeeping service (JA Shin-Iwate, from FY2016).** The JA keeps blue-return (aoiro shinkoku) ledgers and year-end statements for farmers. Cases grew 112 → 156 → 187 (FY2016–18). Fees start at ¥35,000/yr for a household. Farmers hand in cash books monthly, which staff call a burden. Transactions with the JA itself are posted automatically. *(medium)*
   - https://www.jaiwate.or.jp/shin-iwate/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/jiko-kaikaku1811.pdf
   - **This is the closest analogue to DaloyAqua.** Farmers paid for bookkeeping when a co-op they trust delivered it.
9. **JICA two-step loan (1992–95)** lent through 620 PH co-ops and reached about 133k smallholders. The evaluators found weak co-op finances and recommended co-op warehouses and post-harvest facilities. *(medium)*
   - https://www.jica.go.jp/english/our_work/evaluation/oda_loan/post/2000/pdf/02s-12.pdf

## D. Fishpond
10. **FAO PH benchmarks (old, about 1995–99):** tilapia ponds run 0.1–1 ha (fresh or brackish). Semi-intensive stocking is 2–4 fish/m² for 2–6 t/ha/crop. Milkfish ponds run 0.5–8 ha, brackish. For a family pond of ≤0.5 ha, tilapia fits better, **if the water is fresh**. This is a sanity range, not a business case. *(medium)*
    - https://www.fao.org/4/W6928E/w6928e2c.htm

## What this changes for DaloyAqua
- **Association first, not app first.** The evidence (pooled volume, the JA bookkeeping service, CFIDP accounting budgets, community organizing) points to selling bookkeeping through a farmer group, not an app to individual farmers.
- **Assisted, offline-capable entry.** An association bookkeeper or Cowboy's brother enters data for farmers. That fits the connectivity and skills barriers.
- **Log copra moisture and deductions.** Each sale should record moisture method and deduction %, so the visual-grading loss can be measured locally (→ spec 003).
- **The logbook should grow toward co-op-grade financial statements.** Co-ops need these for their CDA Certificate of Compliance (Round 3), and lenders need them too. The sister's management accounting role becomes central.
- **Hold off on the fishpond** until the water source is checked.
